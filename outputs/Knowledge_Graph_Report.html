Entities that recur together across studies are clustered by Leiden community detection; each cluster is summarised into one higher-level concept. This is the emergent, data-driven layer — not a predefined list.

Mechanism_Response  39 members · 16 studies

Structured reasoning uptake

This cluster captures the learner-side response to reasoning aids, examples, feedback, and virtual cases: some supports are adopted and processed, while others are ignored, judged too complex, or seen as offering limited added value. In the realist logic, the mechanism is the learner’s appraisal and use of the offered structure or feedback, which can lead to improved diagnostic estimation, recognition of missing information, repeated correct answers, or illness-script development when the resource is taken up. Where the support feels time-consuming, overly complex, or insufficiently useful, the response is non-use or only limited learning.

Members include: subtle limited added value of SE models, virtual experience of real-time reasoning techniques, error identification and correct-solution comparison, complex individual decision-making situations, learning after failed retrieval, reflection on and improvement of reasoning, familiarity with existing structured tools, time-compressed virtual patient cases
Mechanism_Resource  29 members · 22 studies

Contrastive Diagnostic Scaffolding

This cluster represents a family of teaching resources that structure diagnosis around contrasts: compare-and-contrast prompts, differential tables, feature-oriented instructions, paired examples, and guided combined-strategy practice. In realist terms, these resources work by offering a schema for noticing shared and distinguishing features, which can reorganise knowledge and shift learners toward more structured, combined reasoning; in novices this is associated with improved performance and greater accuracy after contrastive learning. The cluster also includes resources that can create confusion when combined instructions are too dense, showing that the mechanism depends on how the instructional input is framed and used.

Members include: typed feature list with terminology explanation, combined-strategy accuracy hypothesis not supported, analytic cytopathology feature training, novice diagnosticians, increased use of schema-based reasoning, consideration of alternatives and conflicting findings, traditional cardiac physical diagnosis teaching, differential diagnosis evidence table
Intervention  25 members · 12 studies

Reasoning Practice Scaffolding

This cluster represents interventions that make clinical reasoning visible and actionable: students are prompted to query an initial hypothesis, compare it with contradictory or absent findings, and practice reasoning in individual, small-group, seminar, or course-based formats. The resources include explicit modelling/explanation of expert reasoning, teacher-led consultation, video-enriched or virtual patient cases, and bias-focused prompts such as search-satisficing instruction and self-monitoring. In realist terms, these interventions offer structured cues and practice opportunities that can trigger learners to think during hypothesis query, understand physician thought processes, and refine or correct diagnoses.

Members include: CFS case-based seminar, second-diagnosis search, misleading feature or diagnosis suggestion, explicit modelling/explanation of expert reasoning, student engagement in clinical reasoning practice, querying initial diagnostic hypothesis, oversearching for nonexistent second diagnosis, three-consultation clinical reasoning skill framework
Mechanism_Response  23 members · 18 studies

Structured Diagnostic Knowledge Building

This cluster captures a mechanism-response in which learners move from poorly developed illness scripts and weak links to disease mechanisms toward more structured diagnostic knowledge. Worked examples, case-based reflection, self-explanation, and embedded prompts appear to support selective organisation, storage and retrieval of knowledge, detection of misunderstandings, and proposed illness-script enrichment, often with lower cognitive load and greater confidence. The same response can also be seen in delayed-test gains and improved MCQ performance, especially when learners engage diligently with practice and feedback.

Members include: students lacking well-developed illness scripts, logical connection to disease mechanisms, structured diagnostic process schema, natural expert non-analytic abstract reasoning, proposed illness-script enrichment, video-game-experienced surgical learners, lower cognitive load during example study, structured reflection diagnostic case practice
Mechanism_Response  18 members · 8 studies

Pressure-driven case patterning

This cluster captures a response pattern in which learners confronted with simulated ward multitasking, short case windows, multiplied patient encounters, and competing pager demands move toward quicker recognition and more secure case representations. Across the studies, the offered resources include varied cases, case-based diagnosis/explanation, and theory-provided tutorials or games, but the learner response is often frustration, limited perceived benefit from theory, or no discernable difference in approach. The mechanism is the shift from deliberate theoretical processing to implicit learning and faster recognition under pressure, especially for students near transition to junior doctor.

Members include: tutorial-provided theoretical understanding, students near transition to junior doctor, limited perceived benefit from theory, simulated multi-tasking ward experience, varied same-process clinical cases, diagnose explain and learn from cases, implicit learning and faster recognition, difficulty too high for some students
Mechanism_Response  17 members · 14 studies

Unguided Diagnostic Reasoning

This cluster captures a response pattern in which learners are left to diagnose, notice features, or reason spontaneously with little or no explicit learning process guidance. Across the studies, this shows up as minimal exposure, speeded single-diagnosis work, natural-strategy or spontaneous-reasoning instruction, and unguided practice with biasing or incorrect suggestions; the common outcome is weaker or less effective implementation, shorter engagement, missed self-explanation, and lower accuracy. In realist terms, the mechanism-response is the learner’s unguided diagnostic processing, which is especially visible when novices or low-support conditions lead them to rely on whatever strategy they already have rather than a newly scaffolded one.

Members include: self-generated helpful abnormal-cell features, minimal exposure to cytopathology images, no explicit learning processes, speeded single-diagnosis task, simply giving a diagnosis, lower accuracy under incorrect suggestion without guidance, missed additional self-explanation, natural-strategy instruction
Mechanism_Response  17 members · 15 studies

Guided Self-Explanation

This cluster represents a mechanism-response pattern in which learners are given prompts, explicit cognitive-feature focus, or guided consultation steps and then respond by generating explanations, thinking more actively, and keeping their reasoning on track. Across the studies, this response is associated with knowledge elaboration and reorganisation, inference generation, and accelerated acquisition or transfer of targeted reasoning skills, especially when working through written clinical cases with limited prior familiarity. In realist terms, the offered resources appear to elicit a more productive reasoning process that supports diagnostic case solving and consultation performance.

Members include: inference generation and knowledge integration, self-explanation diagnostic case solving, limited familiarity with clinical topic, case reading and self-explanation prompt without feedback, guided performance of consultation reasoning steps, intended insight into cognitive features of reasoning, student performance consultation, written jaundice clinical cases
Outcome  12 members · 13 studies

Error-Resistant Diagnostic Learning

This cluster describes an outcome where learners become less likely to miss the correct diagnosis or be led astray by misleading information, even though their pathophysiology knowledge and deep conceptual understanding do not substantially improve. It appears in settings with difficult far-transfer cases, worked examples containing errors plus KCR feedback, and official diagnosis feedback after each case, alongside unsupported similar-case reasoning with reduced feedback. In realist terms, the offered resources and case demands seem to produce a more error-resistant diagnostic response, while leaving the deeper knowledge base largely unchanged.

Members include: official diagnosis feedback after each case, difficult far-transfer diagnostic cases, insufficient biomedical integration in illness scripts, insufficient deep conceptual understanding, incomplete error-prone prior knowledge, worked examples with errors and KCR feedback, unawareness or incapacity to solve errors, resistance to misleading information
