Methodological progress report · benchmarked against Richmond et al. (2020)

An agentic multi-agent system for realist evidence synthesis

Reproducing the analytic operations of a published realist review, with every claim traced to a verbatim quote. Prepared for review; the system is under active development.

This report explains what the system is for, how it is built, and how well it reproduces a human realist review — Richmond et al.'s (2020) synthesis of educational interventions for clinical reasoning (28 papers). Verification is not a self-assigned grade: we transcribed Richmond's own published concepts, relations, and programme-theory chains into a reference key and checked, item by item, whether the system recovered them and from which quote. Every number below was taken from the run described here.

1What the system is for

Realist synthesis asks not simply whether an intervention works, but what about it works, for whom, and in what circumstances (Pawson & Tilley, 1997). Its unit of analysis is the Context–Mechanism–Outcome configuration (CMOC): within a context, an intervention offers a resource, a learner responds, and an outcome follows. Assembling these across a body of literature and drawing them into a programme theory is slow expert work.

The goal is a system that performs these operations with a human in charge at every judgement point, and that is not tied to one review method: a method-agnostic core with realist synthesis added as a plug-in. Success is defined narrowly — can the system recover the contexts, mechanisms, outcomes, and programme theory a careful human team published, with each element auditable against the source.

2Entities and relationships: definitions and types

The knowledge graph is built from two kinds of object. An entity is a single realist element extracted from a paper and grounded in a verbatim quote; every entity has exactly one of five types. A relationship is a directed, typed causal link between two entities; only five predicates are allowed, each with a fixed domain and range, so the graph stays a coherent C→M→O structure rather than free text.

Table 1. The five relationship predicates and where each may apply.
PredicateFrom (domain)To (range)
PROVIDESInterventionMechanism Resource / Response
TRIGGERSIntervention / Mechanism ResourceMechanism Response / Outcome

LEADS_TOMechanism ResponseMechanism Response / Outcome

A link whose endpoints break these rules is not silently kept — it is demoted to an untyped candidate and surfaced for human review. On this run the graph holds 180 concept-level entities and 439 typed relations.

3Architecture and algorithm

The system runs as a staged pipeline of specialised agents over one shared database — the single source of truth. Narrow agents give more faithful output than one agent asked to do everything, and they mirror the division of labour in a review team. A human sign-off sits at each stage that needs judgement.

Two design choices are worth naming. First, extraction is self-correcting: the extractor drafts, an independent checker in a different model family critiques the draft, and the extractor revises before anything is stored — echoing Richmond's practice of a second reviewer checking every paper. Self-refinement of this kind is established to improve model output (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023). Second, higher-level concepts are discovered, not pre-listed: entities are clustered by Leiden community detection (Traag et al., 2019), the method Microsoft's GraphRAG uses (Edge et al., 2024), and each community is summarised into one "big concept". One empirical caution shaped the design — injecting the full programme theory into the per-paper extraction prompt was found to reduce how many configurations were recovered, so that guidance is kept light and steers naming and synthesis, not the first read.

5How verification works — and what it is not

Richmond's paper is prose, not a scored test. So the standard is built from that prose: we read their Results §3.2 and Figures 2 and 3 and transcribed, by hand, the analytic content they published — 47 concepts (their contexts, interventions, mechanisms and outcomes), 40 causal relations among them, and their 5 context-specific programme-theory chains — each tagged with its location in the paper. This transcription is the reference key.

Verification then compares the system's output to that key one item at a time: for each concept Richmond describes, is there an extracted concept expressing the same thing, and if so from which study and quote? A number such as "43 of 47" is therefore not a grade the system gave itself — it is a count of how many of Richmond's published items were recovered, and every count is traceable to a page in Richmond and a verbatim quote in a source paper (§6). Where the semantic match is a judgement call, it is made by a separate model and flagged for a human to ratify; it is never treated as settled.

7Honest assessment

What works. The system reproduces the inclusion decisions, recovers most of the content Richmond published, and grounds nearly every element in a verbatim quote — so the output can be audited rather than trusted on faith. The causal patterns are almost all present, and the big concepts the graph finds on its own (guided reasoning practice, self-explanation, testing-enhanced study, and so on) are recognisably the mechanisms a human would name. Its strongest programme-theory chain, for low-knowledge novices, corresponds closely to Richmond's.

What is not there yet. Four of Richmond's 47 elements were missed on this run, including the mixed-knowledge-group context and two affective responses. Six matches sit under a different realist role than Richmond assigned, and a few of those are generous — they are flagged for human ratification, not counted as clean. Exact-concept relation matching is low, and the programme-theory correspondence is model-judged and awaits an expert rating. These are specific, understood gaps, not diffuse weakness.

Is the current result strong enough to keep going? We think yes. On a genuinely hard expert task the system already recovers most of a published human review with traceable evidence, and where it falls short the reasons are concrete. Three mechanisms requested for this project — seed-guided extraction, graph-aware extraction, and a human-feedback-to-few-shot loop — are now built in and were active on this run; refining them is the next step.
