How to review and save your decisions
  1. Choose a reference concept or relation on the left. Check the original Richmond passage and its complete conditions; use reference_problem if the reference needs correction.
  2. Search actual system records on the right. Open the candidate, its real endpoints and source evidence. Search is literal text filtering, not semantic matching or ranking.
  3. Record candidate IDs and your verdict in your own A or B CSV. Use equivalent, partial, contradictory, not_recovered, uncertain or reference_problem. Empty cells mean unreviewed, not missing.
  4. For a concept use match_unit=entity. For one relation use single_assertion. For several edges use connected_path and list their IDs in directed order separated by semicolons. Paths must share one paper/configuration and join actual entity IDs.
  5. For equivalent relations assess subject_match, object_match, predicate_match, context_match, qualifiers_match and inference_match as equivalent. Otherwise record equivalent/partial/different/uncertain/not_reported/not_applicable as appropriate. A connected path does not prove matching conditions or semantic support.
  6. Assess source_support separately: supported, partial, unsupported, uncertain or not_assessed. Record source_locator_and_note, reference_scope_note, rationale, reviewer identity, ISO date/time and minutes. Do not use endpoint quotations alone as proof of a relationship.
  7. Keep A/B independent. Adjudicate only after both are completed, recording adjudication_reason. Attribute actual human intervention separately; these forms only evaluate frozen output.

These reference-centered forms cannot estimate source precision over all generated claims. The same output can correspond to the reference and still lack adequate primary-source support. No accuracy score is generated from this page.
